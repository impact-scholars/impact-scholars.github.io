# Impact Scholars Program - 2023 Edition

The inaugural 2023 cohort brought together scholars working on questions in climate science, from fire regimes in <wiki:Patagonia> to vegetation monitoring in the <wiki:Shebelle_River> basin. Their work spans ocean-atmosphere teleconnections, land cover change, and extreme weather events across four continents.

## Climate oscillations and teleconnections

A central theme of the 2023 cohort is the far-reaching influence of [ENSO](wiki:El_Niño–Southern_Oscillation) on regional climate. [Shree et al.](xref:shree-2023#fig-enso-correlations) mapped how ENSO modulates precipitation, sea surface temperature, and air temperature across the Pacific, revealing a characteristic C-shaped region of opposing rainfall anomalies between El Nino and La Nina years. [Ray et al.](xref:ray-2023#fig-enso-upwelling-analysis) investigated how canonical and [Modoki](wiki:El_Niño–Southern_Oscillation#Modoki) El Nino events drive distinct coastal <wiki:Upwelling> patterns off Northwest Africa, with direct consequences for marine productivity. Meanwhile, [Yang et al.](xref:yang-2023#fig-enso-iod-teleconnections) examined how ENSO and the <wiki:Indian_Ocean_Dipole> jointly shape the timing of summer monsoon onset over <wiki:Indonesia> and Northern Australia, finding that the IOD's teleconnection patterns have shifted in recent years.

## Land surface, vegetation and fire

Other groups turned to terrestrial systems and how they respond to environmental pressures. [Barrionuevo et al.](xref:barrionuevo-2023#figure-main) assessed burned area across latitudes in the <wiki:Valdivian_temperate_forests>, finding that the northern portion of the Andean-Patagonian forest experienced the most significant fire impacts between 2001 and 2019. In tropical <wiki:Mato_Grosso>, [Oliveira et al.](xref:oliveira-2023#fig:land-cover-change) traced how rapid agricultural expansion drove a 7% reduction in forested area alongside strong correlations between soy cultivation, GDP, and population growth. [Pozo et al.](xref:pozo-2023#fig-cloud-ndvi-analysis) tackled a methodological challenge in East Africa: cloud cover interference with satellite-based [NDVI](wiki:Normalized_difference_vegetation_index) estimates, showing that regional NDVI may be substantially underestimated during boreal summer.

## Ocean dynamics and extreme events

The remaining projects explored ocean observations and precipitation extremes. [Porteous et al.](xref:porteous-2023#fig-main) compared sea surface height from the [ECCO](wiki:Estimating_the_Circulation_and_Climate_of_the_Ocean) model against tide gauge records during <wiki:Hurricane_Maria>, showing that tide gauges capture localized storm surge better but are vulnerable to physical damage, while the model provides reliable long-term coverage. [Hartzell et al.](xref:hartzell-2023#fig-panels) investigated how oceanic oscillations including the Indian Ocean Dipole and the [Atlantic Nino](wiki:Atlantic_Niño) interact with precipitation in the <wiki:Congo_Basin>, finding complex non-linear relationships. Finally, [Thomas et al.](xref:thomas-2023#fig-spatial-trends) documented a statistically significant upward trend in extreme precipitation events across India from 1901 to 2022, with notable regional differences between the northeast, northwest, central, and southern peninsular regions.
